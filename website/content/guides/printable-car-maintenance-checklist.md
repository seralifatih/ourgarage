Most car problems that leave people stranded give some warning first: a tire slowly losing air, an oil level creeping down, a coolant reservoir going low. A monthly check catches those while they're cheap to fix.

## Section 1: Every month

Do these with the car parked on level ground. Tire pressure and coolant need the car cold, so the morning before you drive works well. For the oil level, follow your manual: many say to check it a few minutes after switching the engine off. The whole list takes 10 to 15 minutes once you're used to it.

1. **Tire pressure, all four plus the spare.** Use your own gauge and the pressure on the placard in the driver's door jamb, not the number on the tire. NHTSA recommends checking at least monthly. Our guide on [how often to check tire pressure](/guides/how-often-to-check-tire-pressure/) explains how to get an accurate reading.
2. **Tire tread and sidewalls.** Look for uneven wear, bulges, cuts, and nails or screws. Use the penny test: if you can see the top of Lincoln's head when the penny goes into the tread head-first, the tread is worn to about 2/32 of an inch and the tire is due for replacement.
3. **Engine oil level.** Pull the dipstick, wipe it, reinsert it fully and read it, following your manual's instructions for when to check. Some cars show the level on the dashboard instead; the manual explains how.
4. **Coolant level.** Check the overflow reservoir is between the marks. Only with the engine cold, and never open a hot radiator cap.
5. **Washer fluid.** Top it up.
6. **All lights and turn signals.** Headlights (low and high), brake lights, tail lights, turn signals and hazards. A reflection in a window or garage door helps if you're alone.
7. **Wiper blades.** Streaks, chattering or torn rubber mean they're due.
8. **Dashboard warning lights.** Start the car and note anything that stays on.
9. **Update your odometer reading.** Write it down or enter it in your maintenance app. It's how you know whether a mileage-based service is coming up.

If a check turns something up, write it down and deal with it before the next month's check. A coolant level that keeps dropping or a tire that keeps needing air is a symptom, not a refill job.

## Section 2: When due

These don't happen every month. Tick the month in which each was done, so the sheet doubles as a simple record for the year:

- Oil and filter change
- Tire rotation
- Engine air filter
- Cabin air filter
- Brake inspection
- Battery test (worth doing yearly once a battery is a few years old)
- Registration or inspection

The intervals for these are in your owner's manual, and they vary a lot between cars. The checklist doesn't print intervals for that reason. For a fuller record with dates, mileage and costs, use the [car maintenance log template](/guides/car-maintenance-log-template/) alongside this checklist.

## Section 3: Before a long trip

Eight items to tick a week or so before you leave, so there's time to fix anything you find:

- Tire pressure set for the load you're carrying (some placards list a higher "loaded" pressure)
- Spare, jack and lug wrench present and the spare inflated
- Whether an oil change will come due during the trip
- Brake fluid level
- Battery age and clean terminals
- A/C and heat both working
- Emergency kit packed
- Registration and insurance in the car

The [road trip checklist](/guides/road-trip-car-checklist/) explains each item.

## Making the habit stick

A checklist only works if you remember to use it. What helps:

- **Pick a fixed day,** like the first Saturday of the month, and put a recurring reminder on your phone.
- **Pair it with something you already do,** such as washing the car or filling up with gas.
- **Keep the gauge and a flashlight in the same place** as the checklist, so you don't have to hunt for them.
- **If more than one person drives the car,** agree who does the check each month. Otherwise everyone assumes someone else did it.
- **Start a new sheet in January.** Keep last year's with your service records.

## When a printed sheet isn't enough

A paper checklist is a good habit builder, but it has blind spots. It can't tell you when the oil change is due by mileage, it only lives in one place, and it can't remind anyone. If you find that the sheet works for the monthly checks but services still sneak up on you, move the service intervals into a reminder app and keep the paper for the monthly walk-around.
