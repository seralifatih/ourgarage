A maintenance log does two jobs. It tells you when the next service is due, and it proves the car was looked after when you sell it or make a warranty claim. The template is set up for both.

## What's on each page

**Page 1: vehicle details and service log.** At the top, write the vehicle's name, year, make and model, plate, VIN and the oil type from your owner's manual. Writing the oil type down saves looking it up every time you buy oil or check a shop's invoice. Below that is the log itself, with 18 rows.

**Page 2: more log rows.** Another 23 rows, so the log lasts. For a car serviced a few times a year, two pages cover several years. Print more copies of page 2 when you need them.

**Page 3: what's due next.** A table listing common items (oil and filter, tire rotation, engine and cabin air filters, brake fluid, coolant, spark plugs, transmission fluid, wiper blades, battery check, registration and inspection) with blank rows for anything else your car needs. For each item you fill in:

- The interval in miles and in months, from your owner's manual
- When it was last done, by date and by odometer reading
- When it's next due, by date and by mileage

## How to fill it in

### The service log

Fill in one row per visit or job, the same day. Waiting a week is how details get lost.

| Column | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Date | The day the work was done | 3/14/2026 |
| Odometer | The reading on the invoice or dashboard | 48,210 |
| Service performed | Each job, briefly | Oil and filter, tire rotation |
| Parts / fluids | Brand, spec, quantity | 0W-20 full synthetic, 5 qt; OEM filter |
| Shop or DIY | Where, or "DIY" | Main Street Auto |
| Cost | Total paid | $89.40 |

Log repairs and one-off jobs too: a new battery, tires, brakes, a windshield, recall work. They're part of the car's history, and a buyer will ask about them.

If you do the work yourself, keep the parts receipt with the log and note the date and mileage. That's your evidence it was done. Our guide to [keeping service records for resale](/guides/car-service-records-for-resale/) covers what buyers and warranty claims look for.

### The "What's due next" page

This is the page that keeps you from going overdue.

1. **Copy the intervals from your owner's manual.** Use the severe schedule if your driving fits it (lots of short trips, stop-and-go traffic, towing, dusty roads, extreme temperatures). For typical ranges, see the [car maintenance schedule by mileage](/guides/car-maintenance-schedule-by-mileage/), but your manual's numbers are the ones to write down.
2. **Fill in "Last done"** for anything you have a record of. If you don't know, write "unknown" and treat it as due soon.
3. **Work out "Next due."** Add the interval to the last-done date and to the last-done mileage. For example, an oil change done on March 14 at 48,210 miles with a 7,500-mile / 12-month interval is next due at 55,710 miles or on March 14 next year.
4. **Whichever comes first wins.** If you drive a lot, the mileage arrives first. If you drive little, the date does.

Each time you log a service on page 1, update the matching row on page 3.

## Tips that make a paper log work

- **Keep it with your receipts**, in one folder, in the house.
- **Write the next due mileage on a sticky note** in the car if you don't have a reminder system. A shop's windshield sticker often shows a generic interval, not your manual's.
- **Check your odometer once a month** and compare it to page 3. A paper log only reminds you if you look at it.
- **Photograph each page** every few months. If the paper is lost, you still have the history.
- **Use a pen.** Pencil fades and smudges, and a log in ink is more convincing to a buyer.

There's also a [printable car maintenance checklist](/guides/printable-car-maintenance-checklist/) for the monthly checks (tire pressure, oil level, lights) that don't need a log row.

## The limits of a paper log

Paper is reliable, but it can't tap you on the shoulder. The most common failure isn't a missing row; it's that nobody checks page 3 until the car is already overdue. It's also stuck in one place: if two people drive the car, only the one holding the folder knows what's been done.

That's where an app helps. You set each interval once, log services from your phone at the shop, and get a notification when something is due by date or mileage. A good setup is to use an app day to day and keep this PDF for a printed record to hand over when you sell.
