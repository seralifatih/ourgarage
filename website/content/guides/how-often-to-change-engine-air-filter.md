<p class="answer"><strong>Short answer:</strong> most engine air filters need replacing every 15,000 to 30,000 miles, and sooner if you drive on dusty, unpaved or construction-heavy roads. Many shops check it at every oil change, and you can check it yourself in a couple of minutes. Your owner's manual gives the interval for your car.</p>

People often ask about "the air filter in a car" without realizing most cars have two. This guide is about the engine air filter, the one that cleans the air your engine breathes. The other one, the cabin air filter, cleans the air coming through your vents; see [how often to change a cabin air filter](/guides/how-often-to-change-cabin-air-filter/).

[[cta]]

## What the engine air filter does

An engine burns a mixture of fuel and air, and it uses a lot of air. The engine air filter sits in a housing in the intake, usually a plastic box near the top or front of the engine, and stops dust, sand, insects and debris from getting into the engine, where they could cause wear.

As the filter does its job, it fills up. Eventually it restricts the airflow, which costs the engine some power.

## How often to replace it

The common range is 15,000 to 30,000 miles. Your manual gives the exact interval, and often a shorter one for dusty conditions.

Replace it sooner if you:

- drive on gravel, dirt or sandy roads,
- live in a dry, dusty area or near construction,
- often drive in heavy traffic with lots of exhaust and road dust.

Because the right interval depends so much on where you drive, the simplest habit is to have it inspected at every [oil change](/guides/how-often-to-change-oil/). Many shops do this as standard and will show you the filter.

## How to check it yourself

1. **Find the air filter housing.** It's usually a black plastic box connected to a large intake hose. The manual shows where.
2. **Open it.** Most use clips; some use screws.
3. **Lift out the filter** and look at it. A new filter is white or off-white. Dark gray or black, with visible dirt packed into the pleats, means it's due.
4. **Hold it up to a light.** If you can barely see light through the pleats, replace it.
5. **Check for damage**, like tears or a warped seal, which let unfiltered air past.
6. **Clean out the housing** of leaves or debris before fitting the new filter, seated the same way as the old one.

## Signs of a clogged filter

- **Sluggish acceleration** or reduced power.
- **A filter that looks dirty** when you check it.
- **Unusual intake sounds**, or debris visible in the housing.

On modern fuel-injected engines, a clogged filter mainly costs performance rather than much fuel economy, because the engine adjusts the fuel to the air it receives. The bigger risk is a damaged or badly seated filter that lets dirt into the engine.

## When to check it sooner

Some situations call for a look at the filter outside the normal interval:

- **After driving through a dust storm**, heavy wildfire smoke or long stretches of unpaved road.
- **If the car has been parked for a long time**, especially outdoors. Rodents sometimes build nests in the air filter housing, and leaves and debris can collect in the intake.
- **After work under the hood**, to make sure the filter and housing were put back properly and sealed.
- **If you notice a sudden drop in power** with no other obvious cause.

Checking takes a couple of minutes and costs nothing, so when in doubt, open the box and look.

## Paper vs. reusable filters

Most cars come with disposable paper filters, which are simply replaced. Some aftermarket filters are washable and reusable, often with an oiled cotton element. If you use one, follow the maker's cleaning and oiling instructions closely; too much oil can foul sensors in the intake. If you're not sure, stick with the type your manual specifies.

## Turbocharged engines

Many modern engines use a turbocharger, which spins at very high speed and depends on clean intake air. A damaged or badly seated air filter can let debris reach the turbo as well as the engine. That makes a correctly fitted, intact filter particularly important on turbocharged cars.

When you change the filter, make sure it sits flat in the housing, all the clips are fastened and no gaps are left around the seal. The owner's manual often notes whether your engine is turbocharged and may give a shorter interval for dusty conditions.

## Engine filter vs. cabin filter

| | Engine air filter | Cabin air filter |
|---|---|---|
| What it cleans | Air going into the engine | Air coming through the vents |
| Usual location | Box on top or front of the engine | Often behind the glovebox |
| What a clogged one does | Reduces engine performance | Weak airflow, musty smell |

Both are cheap and easy to replace, and many people do them together. Write down the date and mileage when you do. For the full picture of what's due when, see the [car maintenance schedule by mileage](/guides/car-maintenance-schedule-by-mileage/).
