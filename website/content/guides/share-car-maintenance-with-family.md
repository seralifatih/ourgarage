<p class="answer"><strong>Short answer:</strong> keep one shared maintenance record for each car that everyone who drives it can see and update, give each car one person who's responsible for booking service, and make sure reminders go to everyone rather than to whoever happened to set them up. The record matters most: when one person logs an oil change, the others need to see it.</p>

Car maintenance is easy to manage when one person drives one car. It gets messy when two partners swap cars, a teenager has started driving, a college student has the old car three states away, or you're helping an elderly parent keep their car running. The usual result is either nobody doing it or two people doing it.

[[cta]]

## What goes wrong when maintenance isn't shared

The problems are predictable:

- **Nobody knows when the last oil change was.** The receipt is in someone's glovebox, and the sticker on the windshield has the shop's generic interval.
- **Double work.** One person buys wiper blades on Saturday; the other already bought some on Wednesday. Or a service gets booked that was done last month.
- **Gaps.** Each person assumes the other one is handling the tire rotation. Neither is.
- **Lost history.** The service history lives in one person's email and memory. When the car is sold, half of it can't be found. (See [keeping service records for resale](/guides/car-service-records-for-resale/).)
- **Reminders to the wrong person.** The person who set the reminder isn't the person driving the car this month.

## Step 1: One record per car, visible to everyone

The fix for most of the above is a single maintenance record per car that every driver can see and add to. It should hold:

- Each service: date, mileage, what was done, who did it, cost
- Upcoming items and when they're due
- The current odometer reading

Ways to do it, from simplest to most automatic:

| Option | Works well for | Weak spot |
|---|---|---|
| Notebook in the glovebox | One car, drivers in the same house | Only visible from inside the car; no reminders |
| Shared spreadsheet or note | Households already using shared docs | No reminders; easy to forget to update from a phone at the shop |
| Shared calendar events | Date-based items like registration renewal | Can't handle mileage-based intervals |
| Maintenance app with household sharing | Several drivers, several cars, or a car far away | Everyone has to install it |

Whichever you choose, the important part is that there's only one. Two partial records are worse than one complete one, because each person trusts their own.

## Step 2: Decide who owns what

Shared visibility doesn't mean shared responsibility for everything. A simple split that works for most households:

- **One owner per car** books services, notices when something is coming due, and makes sure it happens. Usually the main driver, or whoever is best at it.
- **Whoever does a job logs it.** If you take the car in or top up the washer fluid, you add it to the record, ideally before you leave the shop.
- **Everyone reports problems.** A new noise, a warning light or a low tire gets written down or mentioned, even if someone else will deal with it.

Say this out loud once. Most of the friction comes from assumptions, not from people refusing to help.

## Step 3: Set reminders that reach everyone

A maintenance schedule only helps if someone is reminded at the right time. Set up recurring reminders for each car from the owner's manual: oil changes, tire rotations, air filters, brake fluid, wiper blades, and date-based items like registration and inspection. For typical ranges, see the [car maintenance schedule by mileage](/guides/car-maintenance-schedule-by-mileage/).

Two things make reminders work in a household:

1. **Everyone gets them.** If only one phone gets the reminder, it's not a shared system.
2. **Mileage stays current.** Many intervals are in miles, and no reminder knows how far the car has gone unless someone enters it. Agree that whoever drives the car most updates the odometer reading every few weeks, or after a long trip.

## Specific situations

### Partners sharing two cars

The most common setup. Each car gets an owner, both of you see both cars, and both of you log what you do. When you swap cars for the week, the other person can still see what's coming due.

### A teen driver

A new driver is a good time to teach basic checks: tire pressure, oil level, washer fluid, what the dashboard lights mean. Let them log what they check. It builds the habit, and you can see that it's actually being done.

### A car at college or far away

You can't look at the car, so the shared record is everything. Ask the student to update the mileage regularly and to log any service they get. You'll see when the oil change is coming due and can remind them, or book it near campus.

### Helping an aging parent

Many adult children end up managing a parent's car from a distance. A shared record lets you see what's been done and what's due without asking every week. If your parent prefers not to use an app, you can keep the record yourself and log services after they tell you about them.

### Before a family road trip

If several people will drive on a trip, do the pre-trip checks together or agree who does them, and log them. Our [road trip checklist](/guides/road-trip-car-checklist/) covers what to look at.
