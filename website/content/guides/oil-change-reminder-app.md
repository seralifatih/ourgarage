<p class="answer"><strong>Short answer:</strong> a good oil change reminder tracks two limits, mileage and time, and alerts you when either one is reached, because most owner's manuals say "whichever comes first." To set one up you need your interval from the manual, the date and odometer reading of your last oil change, and a way to keep the app's mileage current. Without the last part, a mileage reminder never goes off.</p>

Plenty of apps can remind you about an oil change. Most of the reminders that fail do so for one of three reasons: they only track time, they only track miles, or the mileage they know about is months out of date.

[[cta]]

## What an oil change reminder needs to do

### Track both limits

Oil ages with time as well as use. A typical manual interval looks like "every 7,500 miles or 12 months, whichever comes first." Your numbers may differ; see [how often to change your oil](/guides/how-often-to-change-oil/) for where to find them and when the severe schedule applies.

- **Date only** (a calendar event): works if you drive little, but a high-mileage driver hits the mileage limit long before the date.
- **Mileage only** (the windshield sticker): works if you drive a lot, but a low-mileage car can sit for well over a year without reaching it.
- **Both:** the reminder fires at whichever comes first. This is the one you want.

### Know your current mileage

An app can't see your odometer unless your car is connected to it. For most apps, the mileage it knows is the last number you typed in. If you entered it at your last oil change and never since, a mileage reminder will never fire.

Good apps solve this by asking you for the odometer reading every so often. A quick update every few weeks is enough. If you just took a long trip, update it then.

### Start from the real last change

The next due point is calculated from when the oil was last changed: date and mileage. Take both from the shop invoice or your log. If you can't find them, the windshield sticker often has the date and mileage of the last change. If you truly don't know, get the oil changed and start fresh from that day.

### Reach the right person

If two people drive the car, the reminder should reach whoever is going to deal with it. A reminder on one person's phone doesn't help if the other person drives the car most.

## Setting it up, step by step

These steps work in most maintenance apps. We'll use OurGarage as the example since we know it best.

1. **Add the car** with a nickname and its current odometer reading.
2. **Create a reminder** of type "Oil change."
3. **Enter both intervals** from your owner's manual: the distance (for example, 7,500 miles) and the months (for example, 12).
4. **Enter when it was last done:** the date and the odometer reading at that oil change.
5. **Check the result.** The app shows the next due point, such as "Due in 2,300 mi" or a due month, whichever is closer.
6. **Keep the mileage current.** OurGarage reminds you to update the odometer when you have mileage-based reminders. You enter one number and every mileage reminder for that car moves forward.
7. **When the oil is changed,** log the service with the date and mileage. The reminder restarts from there.

In OurGarage you get a notification the morning a reminder falls due, and anyone in your household with the app sees the same reminder.

## Phone calendar vs. a maintenance app

| | Calendar or Reminders | Maintenance app |
|---|---|---|
| Time-based reminder | Yes | Yes |
| Mileage-based reminder | No | Yes, from odometer readings you enter |
| Restarts from the actual service date | Only if you edit it | Yes, when you log the service |
| Service history | No | Yes |
| Other jobs (tires, filters) | One event each, by date only | Yes |
| Cost | Free | Often free for one car |

If you drive very little, a repeating calendar event every 6 or 12 months covers the time limit well. Our guide to [low-mileage car maintenance](/guides/low-mileage-car-maintenance/) explains why time limits matter more for those cars. For everyone else, a reminder that also tracks mileage avoids the "it's been 11,000 miles since the last change" surprise.

## Other reminders worth setting at the same time

Once you've got the oil change reminder working, the same setup handles other items. Add them while you have the owner's manual open:

- Tire rotation (by mileage, often in step with oil changes)
- Engine and cabin air filters
- Brake fluid (often by time)
- Wiper blades (by time)
- Registration and inspection (by date)

If you're still choosing an app, our [comparison of car maintenance apps](/guides/best-app-to-track-car-maintenance/) covers which suits which kind of driver.

## If a reminder fires and you can't get to it right away

Book the oil change for the next few days. A short overrun now and then is far less of a concern than making a habit of it. Check the oil level in the meantime, since an engine that uses some oil will be at its lowest just before a change.
