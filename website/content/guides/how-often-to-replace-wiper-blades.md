<p class="answer"><strong>Short answer:</strong> most windshield wiper blades need replacing every six to twelve months. The rubber ages from sun, heat, cold and ice even when you rarely use the wipers, so replace them as soon as they streak, chatter or skip, whatever the calendar says. Check your owner's manual or the blade maker's fitting guide for the right sizes.</p>

Wipers are a small part with a big effect. You don't notice how much you rely on them until a sudden downpour on the highway, when a worn blade leaves you looking through smears.

[[cta]]

## Why wiper blades wear out

A wiper blade is a strip of rubber pressed against the glass. Its edge needs to stay sharp and flexible to clear water in a thin, even sweep. Over time:

- **Sunlight and heat** harden and crack the rubber.
- **Cold and ice** stiffen it and can tear the edge.
- **Dirt and grit** on the windshield wear the edge down.
- **Ozone and pollution** degrade rubber slowly, even when the car is parked.

That's why age matters as much as use. A car that's rarely driven still needs new wipers; see [low-mileage car maintenance](/guides/low-mileage-car-maintenance/) for other items that age by the calendar.

## Signs it's time to replace them

- **Streaks** or lines of water left behind.
- **Smearing**, especially at night or in oncoming headlights.
- **Chattering or skipping** across the glass.
- **Squeaking.**
- **Unwiped patches**, often where the blade has lifted or bent.
- **Cracked, torn or hardened rubber** when you look closely at the edge.
- **A bent frame or arm.**

Run your finger gently along the rubber edge. It should feel smooth and flexible, not rough, nicked or stiff.

## Choosing replacement blades

- **Get the right sizes.** The driver's and passenger's blades are often different lengths. The owner's manual, the old blades, or a parts store's fitting guide will give the sizes.
- **Conventional, beam or hybrid blades.** Conventional blades have a metal frame; beam blades are a single curved piece that tends to hold more even pressure and collect less ice; hybrids combine the two. Any of them works if it fits your car properly.
- **Winter blades** have a rubber boot that keeps ice out of the frame. They're worth considering where winters are harsh.
- **Don't forget the rear wiper** if your car has one.

## Making wiper blades last longer

- **Keep the windshield clean.** Dirt and grit wear the rubber quickly.
- **Use washer fluid**, not dry wiping. Running wipers on dry glass drags grit across both blade and windshield.
- **Clear ice and snow by hand** before switching the wipers on, and make sure the blades aren't frozen to the glass.
- **Wipe the blade edge** with a damp cloth now and then to remove grime.
- **Park in the shade** when you can, to reduce sun damage.

## Washer fluid

New blades only work well with a clean windshield, and washer fluid is what keeps it that way. A few points:

- **Use proper washer fluid, not plain water.** Washer fluid cleans better, and in cold climates plain water can freeze in the reservoir, lines and nozzles, which leaves you unable to clear salt spray or slush and can crack the reservoir.
- **Match the fluid to the season.** Winter fluids are rated to resist freezing; summer and bug-remover fluids clean better in warm weather but may not protect against frost. Check the temperature rating on the bottle.
- **Keep the reservoir topped up.** It's one of the easiest monthly checks, and you'll go through it fast on highways and salted winter roads.
- **Check the nozzles.** If the spray is weak or misses the glass, the nozzles may be blocked or misaligned.

## Fitting them

Most blades clip onto the wiper arm in a few seconds, and the package usually shows how. Before removing the old blade, lift the arm and never let it spring back with no blade attached: the bare metal arm can crack the windshield. Fit the new blade while holding the arm up, then lower it gently onto the glass. Some cars have a service position that raises the wipers clear of the hood; the manual explains how to use it.

## When the problem isn't the blade

If new blades still chatter or skip, look at the arm and the glass:

- **Weak arm spring.** The arm presses the blade onto the glass. A tired spring lets the blade lift at speed.
- **Bent arm.** If the blade doesn't sit square to the glass, it chatters. A shop can check the angle.
- **Coated or dirty glass.** Wax, oily road film and some glass treatments cause streaking until the windshield is properly cleaned.
- **Chips and cracks** in the wiper's path wear blades quickly and can spread. Get them assessed.

## Remembering them

Wipers have no warning light and no mileage interval, which is why they often get replaced only after they've failed in the rain. Two easy habits:

- **Replace them on a fixed date**, like the start of fall before the wet season, or every six months.
