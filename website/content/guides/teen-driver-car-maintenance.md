<p class="answer"><strong>Short answer:</strong> teach a new driver the monthly checks they can do themselves (tire pressure and tread, oil level, fluids, lights, wipers, warning lights) and have them log each one. Parents keep ownership of scheduled services and anything safety-critical, like brakes and tires, at least at first. Use one shared maintenance record for the car so you can both see what's been done and what's due. The same split works for spouses: one person owns the schedule, both log what they do.</p>

A new driver doesn't yet know what normal looks like for a car. Slow changes like a soft tire or a dropping oil level are easy to miss when you've only been driving for a few months, and easy not to mention when you do notice.

[[cta]]

## What a new driver should learn first

These are the checks worth teaching in the first month. Do them together the first time, then let them do it alone and log it.

### Tires

- **Pressure:** use a gauge, with the tires cold, and fill to the pressure on the placard in the driver's door jamb, not the number on the tire. Monthly, and before long drives. Our guide on [checking tire pressure](/guides/how-often-to-check-tire-pressure/) walks through it.
- **Tread:** the penny test. Put a penny in the tread, Lincoln's head down. If the top of his head is visible, the tread is worn out.
- **Damage:** bulges, cuts, nails. A bulge in the sidewall means don't drive on it.
- **The spare:** where it is, how to get it out, and whether it's inflated. Change a tire together once in the driveway before they need to do it on a road shoulder.

### Fluids

- Oil level on the dipstick (or the dashboard, on cars without one)
- Coolant in the overflow reservoir, only when the engine is cold
- Washer fluid

### Lights and wipers

Walk around the car with the lights on. Wipers that streak or chatter need replacing.

### Dashboard warning lights

Go through the owner's manual together. The essential distinction: a red warning light means a serious problem, so pull over safely and check the manual. For oil pressure or overheating, that means stopping the engine. Amber lights mean get it checked soon. The tire pressure light means check all four tires today.

### What to do when something goes wrong

Save the roadside assistance number in their phone, make sure registration and insurance are in the car, and agree on the rule: if something feels, sounds or smells wrong, they call you before they keep driving.

## Who handles what

A split that works for many families:

| Task | New driver | Parent |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly checks (tires, fluids, lights) | Does them, logs them | Spot-checks now and then |
| Updating the odometer reading | Monthly | Reminds if it's stale |
| Washer fluid, wiper blades | Handles | |
| Oil changes and scheduled services | Takes the car in once they're ready | Owns the schedule, books it |
| Tires and brakes | Reports problems | Decides and pays |
| Warning lights, noises, leaks | Reports immediately | Decides what happens next |

As they gain experience, move more of the right-hand column to the left. The goal is a driver who handles their own car by the time they leave home.

## One shared record

The reason to share a single record, rather than each of you keeping your own notes, is that the car's history only works if it's complete:

- When your teen checks the tires and logs it, you see it without asking.
- When you book an oil change, they see when it's due and can plan around it.
- If they take the car to college, you can still see what's coming up.
- When the car is sold, the full history is in one place.

Mileage is the piece teens forget. Mileage-based reminders only work if someone updates the odometer reading, so make that part of the monthly check.

## The same idea, for spouses and partners

Couples who share cars run into the same problems on a smaller scale. Each person assumes the other handled it, or both handle it. The fix is the same:

- **One owner per car** who keeps an eye on the schedule and books services.
- **Whoever does something logs it,** whether that's an oil change, new wipers or a tire pressure top-up.
- **Both see the same record and reminders,** so it doesn't matter who drove the car this week.
