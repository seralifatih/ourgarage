<p class="answer"><strong>Short answer:</strong> most cars need a new cabin air filter every 15,000 to 30,000 miles or about once a year, whichever comes first. If you drive in dusty areas, heavy traffic or a high-pollen region, change it more often. Your owner's manual gives the exact interval, and on many cars you can replace it yourself in a few minutes.</p>

The cabin air filter, also called the pollen filter, cleans the air that comes through your vents. It's one of the cheapest parts on the car and one of the easiest to forget, because nothing about the engine tells you it's due.

[[cta]]

## What the cabin air filter does

Air comes into the car from outside, usually near the base of the windshield, and passes through the cabin filter before it reaches the vents. The filter traps dust, pollen, leaves and other particles. Some filters add a layer of activated carbon to reduce odors and fumes.

Most modern cars have one, though some older and some base-model cars don't. Your owner's manual will say whether yours has a cabin filter and where it is.

It's separate from the engine air filter, which cleans the air going into the engine. The two are often changed at similar intervals, but they're different parts in different places.

## How often to change it

The common range is 15,000 to 30,000 miles, or once a year. Some manuals give a time interval only, some a mileage, some both.

Change it more often if:

- you drive on dirt or gravel roads,
- you spend a lot of time in heavy traffic,
- you live somewhere with high pollen counts or frequent wildfire smoke,
- anyone in the car has allergies, or
- the car is parked under trees and collects leaves near the air intake.

A once-a-year change, timed for spring before pollen season, is easy to remember.

## Signs it needs changing

- **Weak airflow** from the vents even on the highest fan setting.
- **A musty or dusty smell** when the fan or air conditioning comes on.
- **Windows that fog up** and take longer to clear.
- **More dust** on the dashboard and vents.
- **Allergy symptoms** that are worse in the car.

Some of these can have other causes, such as a failing blower motor or a problem with the air conditioning. A new filter is the cheap first thing to try.

## When it isn't the filter

A new cabin filter fixes most weak-airflow and dusty-smell complaints, but not all of them:

- **Weak airflow on every setting, even with a new filter**, can point to a failing blower motor or its resistor, or a blocked air intake.
- **Air coming from the wrong vents** usually means a stuck blend or mode door, not the filter.
- **A musty smell when the air conditioning starts** often comes from moisture on the AC evaporator, not the filter. Some drivers switch the AC off and leave the fan running for the last few minutes of a drive to help it dry out; your manual may have its own advice.
- **A sweet smell inside the car, or an oily film on the inside of the windshield,** can mean a coolant leak from the heater core. Coolant fumes shouldn't be breathed, and low coolant can overheat the engine, so have it checked soon. It's not a filter problem.

If a new filter doesn't help, mention these symptoms to a shop rather than buying another filter.

## Where it is and how to replace it

On many cars the cabin filter sits behind the glovebox. Typically you empty the glovebox, release its stops so it swings down, and remove a small cover to slide the old filter out. On other cars it's under the hood near the windshield, or under the dashboard.

The owner's manual usually shows the exact steps. A few tips:

- **Note the airflow arrow.** Filters have an arrow showing which way the air should flow. Fit the new one the same way as the old.
- **Use the right size and type.** Carbon filters cost a little more and may help with odors and fumes; plain particle filters work for many drivers. Choose one that fits your car's housing.
- **Clear the area.** If there are leaves or debris around the filter housing or the outside air intake, remove them while you're there.

If you'd rather a shop did it, ask to see the old filter. A dirty filter is gray or black and may have leaves trapped in it.

## Fitting it into your routine

Because the cabin filter has no warning light, it gets forgotten. Two easy ways to remember it:

- **Pair it with another service.** Many people ask for it at every other [oil change](/guides/how-often-to-change-oil/), or at the oil change closest to spring.
- **Put it on a list.** Our [printable car maintenance checklist](/guides/printable-car-maintenance-checklist/) has a row for it, and the [car maintenance schedule by mileage](/guides/car-maintenance-schedule-by-mileage/) shows where it fits alongside everything else.

Note the date and mileage when you change it, so next year's reminder starts from the real date.
