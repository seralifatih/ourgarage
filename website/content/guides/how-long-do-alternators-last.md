<p class="answer"><strong>Short answer:</strong> alternators don't have a service interval, and many last for much of a car's life. Lifespans of roughly 7 to 10 years are often quoted, but heat, heavy electrical loads and a worn drive belt can shorten that. Your owner's manual won't list a replacement mileage; instead, watch for the warning signs below and have the charging system tested if you see them.</p>

The alternator is the part that keeps the battery charged and powers the car's electrics while the engine runs. When it starts to fail, the symptoms are easy to blame on the battery, which is why it's worth knowing the difference.

[[cta]]

## What the alternator does

The engine turns the alternator through a drive belt. The alternator generates electricity to run the lights, the infotainment system, the heated seats and every other electrical system, and to recharge the battery after starting.

The battery's main job is starting the engine. Once the engine is running, the alternator does the work. If the alternator fails, the car keeps running on battery power only until the battery is drained.

## What shortens alternator life

- **Heat.** The alternator sits in a hot engine bay.
- **Electrical load.** Lots of high-draw accessories, aftermarket audio or lighting, or frequent short trips with everything switched on all ask more of it.
- **A weak battery.** A battery that can't hold a charge makes the alternator work harder.
- **The drive belt.** A worn or loose belt can slip and reduce charging, and a belt that's too tight can wear the alternator's bearings.
- **Contamination** from oil or coolant leaks onto the alternator.

## Signs of a failing alternator

- **The battery warning light** on the dashboard while driving. Despite the symbol, it usually means a charging problem.
- **Dim or flickering headlights and dashboard lights**, especially when you use other electrics.
- **Electrical glitches**: power windows slowing down, the radio resetting, warning lights coming and going.
- **A battery that keeps going flat**, even after charging or replacing it.
- **A whining or grinding noise** from the front of the engine that changes with engine speed.
- **A burning smell**, which can come from an overheating alternator or a slipping belt.
- **The car stalling** while driving, once the battery is drained.

## Battery or alternator?

The two are often confused because a failing alternator ends with a dead battery. A rough way to tell them apart:

| Symptom | More likely |
|---|---|
| Slow cranking or no start, but runs fine once started | Battery |
| Battery light on while driving | Alternator or charging system |
| Lights dim or flicker while driving | Alternator |
| New battery goes flat again within days | Alternator, or an electrical drain |

A shop can test both quickly. With a voltmeter, a running engine typically shows somewhere around 13.5 to 14.5 volts at the battery when the alternator is charging; a reading close to the battery's resting voltage while running suggests it isn't charging. Treat this as a hint and have it confirmed. Our guide to [how long car batteries last](/guides/how-long-do-car-batteries-last/) covers the battery side.

## Can a bad alternator damage the battery?

Yes, in two ways. If the alternator stops charging, the car drains the battery completely, and repeated deep discharges shorten a battery's life. If the alternator's voltage regulator fails the other way and overcharges, the battery can overheat and be damaged.

That's why, after an alternator failure, it's worth having the battery tested too, and why replacing a battery that keeps going flat doesn't fix the problem if the alternator is the cause. A shop testing the charging system checks both at once.

## What to do if the battery light comes on

1. **Turn off what you can**: heated seats, rear defroster, the radio.
2. **Avoid stopping the engine** unless you need to; it may not restart.
3. **Head somewhere safe** to have it checked, without planning a long drive.

Before a long trip, a failing alternator is one of the things worth catching early; a charging test fits naturally into our [road trip checklist](/guides/road-trip-car-checklist/).

## Jump-starting with a failing alternator

A jump-start gets a car with a flat battery running, but if the alternator isn't charging, the car will run on what's in the battery and stall again, often within a short drive. If a car needs a jump and then dies again soon after, suspect the charging system rather than the battery alone.

If you must drive it to a shop, keep the trip short, switch off everything you can, and avoid stopping the engine on the way.

## Replacing an alternator

Shops replace alternators with new or remanufactured units. Ask what warranty comes with the part. It's also worth having the drive belt and battery checked at the same time, since a worn belt or weak battery can shorten the new alternator's life.

Keep the invoice. An alternator replacement is a meaningful repair that a later buyer will want to see; see [keeping service records for resale](/guides/car-service-records-for-resale/).
